## Can you catch malaria from another person?

No. Malaria is not contagious and cannot spread from one person to another. It is passed on through the bites of infected female Anopheles mosquitoes. If several people in one home have malaria, each of them was bitten by an infected mosquito.

Source: who-malaria-qa

## Does drinking dirty water cause malaria?

No. Malaria is not caused by drinking dirty water. It comes from the bite of an infected mosquito. Standing water matters for a different reason: mosquitoes breed in it, and most drains and puddles of stagnant water in Nigeria are found to contain the young of malaria mosquitoes.

Source: who-malaria-qa, nmep-ivm

## Does rain or being beaten by rain cause malaria?

No. Rain itself does not give anyone malaria. Malaria is spread by mosquitoes. The link with rain is that rainfall creates more places for mosquitoes to breed, so the risk of malaria is highest in the rainy season.

Source: who-malaria-qa, bakare-2026-adamawa

## Do all mosquitoes carry malaria?

No. Only female Anopheles mosquitoes spread malaria. There are more than 400 species of Anopheles mosquito and only around 40 of them can transmit the disease.

Source: who-malaria-qa

## Does a fever always mean malaria?

No. Fever is one of the most common early symptoms of malaria, but malaria symptoms are not specific and many other illnesses cause fever too. A malaria test is how health workers tell malaria apart from other causes of fever. Treating every fever as malaria can mean the real illness is missed.

Source: who-malaria, who-malaria-qa

## Can herbs, agbo or home remedies cure malaria?

No. Malaria is a serious infection and always requires treatment with medicine. The World Health Organization does not support the use of Artemisia plant material, whether as teas, tablets or capsules, to prevent or treat malaria. Relying on home remedies delays real treatment, and untreated malaria can become severe within 24 hours.

Source: who-malaria, who-malaria-qa

## Can cold weather or cold food cure malaria?

No. Malaria is caused by a parasite in the body, and it always requires treatment with malaria medicine. What a person eats or drinks, or how warm or cold they are, does not remove the parasite.

Source: who-malaria

## If I have had malaria before, am I immune?

No. People who live where malaria is common may develop partial immunity, which can make later infections milder, but it never gives complete protection. People who have had malaria can get it again and should keep using prevention such as mosquito nets.

Source: who-malaria-qa

## Are mosquito nets harmful or unnecessary?

Insecticide-treated nets are one of the two core tools the World Health Organization recommends against malaria. They prevent bites while people sleep and kill mosquitoes as they try to feed. WHO assesses mosquito control products for safety, effectiveness and quality.

Source: who-malaria, who-malaria-qa

## Does the malaria vaccine give full protection?

No. WHO describes the malaria vaccines as safe and effective and says they significantly reduce malaria and deadly severe malaria in young children. They work best together with other measures such as bed nets, so a vaccinated child still needs to sleep under a net.

Source: who-malaria
