## What is malaria?

Malaria is a life-threatening disease caused by a parasite. It is spread to people by the bites of some infected female Anopheles mosquitoes and is mostly found in tropical countries. Malaria is preventable and curable.

Source: who-malaria

## How does malaria spread?

Malaria spreads mostly through the bite of an infected female Anopheles mosquito. It is not contagious and does not pass from one person to another, so you cannot catch malaria by being near or touching someone who has it. Blood transfusion and contaminated needles may also transmit malaria.

Source: who-malaria, who-malaria-qa

## Can malaria be prevented and cured?

Yes. Malaria can be prevented by avoiding mosquito bites and with medicines, and it is a treatable disease. Without prompt diagnosis and effective treatment, a mild case of malaria can progress to a severe form of the disease, which is often fatal without treatment.

Source: who-malaria, who-malaria-qa

## Which parasites cause malaria?

Five species of Plasmodium parasite cause malaria in humans. Two of them, Plasmodium falciparum and Plasmodium vivax, pose the greatest threat. P. falciparum is the deadliest malaria parasite and the most common one in Africa. P. vivax is the dominant malaria parasite in most countries outside sub-Saharan Africa. The other species that can infect humans are P. malariae, P. ovale and P. knowlesi.

Source: who-malaria

## How common is malaria worldwide?

Nearly half of the world's population is at risk of malaria. The World Health Organization estimates that in 2024 there were 282 million malaria cases and about 610,000 malaria deaths in 80 countries. These figures are updated every year.

Source: who-malaria, who-malaria-qa

## Where in the world is malaria most common?

Malaria is found mainly in tropical and subtropical countries, and the WHO African Region carries most of the burden. In 2024 the African Region had about 95% of all malaria cases and about 95% of all malaria deaths. Children under 5 years old accounted for about three quarters of malaria deaths in the Region.

Source: who-malaria, who-malaria-qa

## When is the risk of malaria highest?

The risk of malaria is higher in some areas than others. It depends on several factors, including the type of local mosquitoes. The risk can also change with the season, and in tropical countries it is highest during the rainy season.

Source: who-malaria-qa

## Does having malaria before make you immune?

No. Some people who live where malaria is common develop partial immunity after long exposure. Partial immunity never gives complete protection, but it lowers the risk that a malaria infection will become severe. This is why most malaria deaths in Africa are in young children, who have not yet built up this partial immunity. People who have had malaria before can get it again.

Source: who-malaria-qa
