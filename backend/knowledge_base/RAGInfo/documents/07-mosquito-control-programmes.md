## What is vector control for malaria?

Vector control means controlling the mosquitoes that spread malaria. It is the main approach to preventing malaria and reducing its spread. The two core vector control measures are insecticide-treated nets and indoor residual spraying.

Source: who-malaria, who-malaria-qa

## What is indoor residual spraying?

Indoor residual spraying, or IRS, is the application of an insecticide to the surfaces where mosquitoes tend to rest, such as the inside walls, eaves and ceilings of houses. In Nigeria, IRS is planned for targeted areas under the national malaria programme.

Source: who-malaria-qa, nmep-strategic-plan-2021-2025

## Why does standing water matter for malaria?

Mosquitoes that spread malaria breed in water. Nigeria's National Malaria Elimination Programme reports that most drains and puddles of stagnant water are found to contain Anopheles larvae, the young stage of the mosquito. Reducing mosquito breeding sites is one of the aims of Nigeria's mosquito control strategy.

Source: nmep-ivm

## What is larviciding?

Larviciding means treating water where mosquitoes breed so that the young mosquitoes do not survive. It has to be carefully matched to local conditions, the type of water and the local mosquitoes, and it needs regular, repeated application because it has little lasting effect. Nigeria's programme emphasises products that are safe for the environment and for drinking water sources. Larviciding is planned and carried out by public health programmes.

Source: nmep-ivm

## How does Nigeria organise mosquito control?

Integrated Vector Management is the main mosquito control strategy in Nigeria. It uses different control methods alone or together to reduce mosquito numbers and contact between people and mosquitoes. It includes insecticidal nets, protective clothing, repellents, indoor residual spraying, larviciding and environmental control. Ministries responsible for agriculture, water resources, environment, works and housing, and education are involved.

Source: nmep-ivm

## What is insecticide resistance and why does it matter?

Insecticide resistance means mosquitoes are no longer killed as easily by an insecticide. WHO warns that growing resistance among Anopheles mosquitoes threatens progress against malaria. In Nigeria, monitoring found resistance to pyrethroids, the insecticides most used on nets, to be widespread at all monitoring sites. New-generation nets give better protection than nets treated only with pyrethroids and are becoming more widely available. Sleeping under a net is still recommended.

Source: who-malaria, nmep-strategic-plan-2021-2025

## What is Anopheles stephensi?

Anopheles stephensi is an invasive mosquito species that can spread malaria. It is originally from parts of south Asia and the Arabian Peninsula and has been expanding its range, with detections reported in eight African countries. It thrives in urban settings, tolerates high temperatures and is resistant to many of the insecticides used in public health.

Source: who-malaria
