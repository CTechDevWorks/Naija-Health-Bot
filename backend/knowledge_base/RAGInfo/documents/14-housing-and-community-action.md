## Do window screens help prevent malaria?

Yes. Using window screens is one of the ways the World Health Organization lists to lower the risk of mosquito bites, along with mosquito nets, repellents and protective clothing.

Source: who-malaria

## How can a house be improved to keep mosquitoes out?

Simple changes to a house can reduce the number of mosquitoes that get in. These include screening windows and doors, closing the gaps under the eaves, fitting a ceiling, and sealing cracks and holes in walls. If these changes are not possible, sleeping under a mosquito net every night matters even more.

Source: who-housing-vector, who-malaria

## How can a household reduce mosquito breeding around the home?

Keep the area around the home free of standing water. Remove rubbish that collects water, such as old tyres, cans and broken containers. Empty or turn over containers that are not in use, keep water storage drums and tanks tightly covered, and clear gutters and drains so that water flows.

Source: nmep-ivm, who-lsm-manual

## What can a community do together against malaria?

Neighbours can organise clean-up days to clear blocked drains, remove rubbish that holds water and fill in puddles that keep coming back. Large problems, such as a blocked public drain or a flooded area that does not drain, should be reported to the local government health or environment office. Community action works best alongside sleeping under nets and prompt testing for fever.

Source: nmep-ivm, who-malaria

## Should households spray or treat water with chemicals themselves?

No. Spraying insecticides widely or treating water with chemicals to kill mosquitoes is work for trained and authorised public health teams with protective equipment. Households should not mix or apply these chemicals themselves and should never put chemicals into water used for drinking, cooking or washing.

Source: who-lsm-manual, nmep-ivm

## Who is responsible for mosquito control in Nigeria?

Mosquito control in Nigeria is led by the National Malaria Elimination Programme through Integrated Vector Management. Other ministries work with it, including those responsible for agriculture, water resources, environment, works and housing, and education.

Source: nmep-ivm

## Is cleaning the environment enough to prevent malaria?

No. Keeping the surroundings free of standing water reduces mosquito breeding but does not stop malaria on its own. It should be combined with sleeping under a mosquito net, avoiding bites after dusk, and getting tested and treated promptly for fever.

Source: who-malaria
