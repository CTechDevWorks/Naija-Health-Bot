## Why is there more malaria in the rainy season?

In tropical countries the risk of malaria is highest during the rainy season. Rainfall creates more places for mosquitoes to breed and increases the number of mosquitoes, which raises the chance of malaria being passed on.

Source: who-malaria-qa, bakare-2026-adamawa

## How long after the rains does malaria increase?

Malaria cases tend to rise some weeks after the rain, not immediately. A study of monthly data from Adamawa State, Nigeria, covering 2015 to 2024 found the strongest link about two months after rainfall and about one month after changes in temperature. The delay reflects the time mosquitoes need to multiply and the parasite needs to develop. These timings come from one state and may differ in other places.

Source: bakare-2026-adamawa

## How do temperature and humidity affect malaria?

Temperature, rainfall and humidity all affect how mosquitoes live and how quickly the malaria parasite develops inside the mosquito. In the Adamawa State study, malaria cases followed a clear yearly pattern, with peaks in the rainy season when conditions were most favourable for mosquitoes and the parasite.

Source: bakare-2026-adamawa

## Does the rainy season mean a fever is malaria?

No. The season changes how much malaria there is in an area, but it cannot show whether one person has malaria. Malaria symptoms look like those of other illnesses, so a fever needs a malaria test at any time of year.

Source: who-malaria, who-malaria-qa

## Is weather the only thing that changes malaria levels?

No. The Adamawa State study found that malaria levels are also shaped by things other than climate, including changes in mosquito control measures, access to health care, and changes to the environment such as land use or irrigation.

Source: bakare-2026-adamawa

## When are the malaria seasons in different parts of Nigeria?

In Nigeria the malaria season mostly follows the rains. In the mangrove and rainforest zones of the south, rain falls throughout the year. In the Guinea and Sudan savannah there are two rainfall peaks, usually starting in March or April, with a break in August, then continuing until October. In the Sahel savannah of the far north the rains start around June or July and last about three to four months.

Source: nmep-strategic-plan-2021-2025
