## Who is at higher risk of severe malaria?

Infants, children under 5 years, pregnant women and girls, travellers, and people with HIV or AIDS are at higher risk of severe malaria infection.

Source: who-malaria

## Why are young children most at risk from malaria?

People who live where malaria is common can build up partial immunity after long exposure, which lowers the risk of severe disease. Young children have not yet built this up, which is why most malaria deaths in Africa are in young children. Children under 5 accounted for about three quarters of malaria deaths in the WHO African Region in 2024.

Source: who-malaria, who-malaria-qa

## Why are people who move into malaria areas at higher risk?

People who have no partial immunity to malaria are at higher risk. This includes travellers from countries without malaria, migrants and mobile populations, and also people within a malaria-affected country who live in areas with little or no malaria and then go to an area with high transmission.

Source: who-malaria-qa

## Are people living with HIV at higher risk from malaria?

Yes. People with HIV or AIDS are at particular risk of developing severe malaria.

Source: who-malaria, who-malaria-qa

## Does sickle cell protect against malaria?

People who carry one sickle cell gene (sickle cell trait) have some natural protection against severe malaria, but they can still get malaria. People who have sickle cell disease are not protected and can become very ill if they get malaria, so prevention and prompt testing matter even more for them.

Source: PENDING

## If I am not in a high-risk group, can I still get malaria?

Yes. Partial immunity never gives complete protection, and in areas with less malaria and low immunity all age groups are at risk. Anyone with a fever in a place where malaria is present should get tested promptly.

Source: who-malaria-qa
