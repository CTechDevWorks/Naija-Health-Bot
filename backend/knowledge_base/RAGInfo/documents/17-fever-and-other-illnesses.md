## Is every fever malaria?

No. Fever, headache and chills are common in malaria, but they are also common in many other illnesses. Malaria cannot be recognised reliably from symptoms. A malaria test lets health workers tell malaria apart from other causes of fever and give the right treatment.

Source: who-malaria, who-malaria-qa

## What should someone with a fever do in a place where malaria is common?

Get tested promptly. WHO recommends prompt diagnosis for anyone with suspected malaria, because P. falciparum malaria can progress to severe illness if it is not treated within 24 hours.

Source: who-malaria-qa

## What else commonly causes fever in Nigeria?

Fever has many causes. A multi-pathogen study of people with sudden fever in Nigeria found a wide range of infections, and coinfections were common and hard to tell apart from a single infection by symptoms alone. Only a health worker, with tests, can tell these illnesses apart.

Source: rti-safian-fever

## What if the malaria test is negative but the person is still sick?

A negative malaria test means another cause of the illness should be looked for. The person should go back to a health worker and not take malaria medicine anyway. If the fever continues, the health worker may repeat the malaria test and check for other illnesses.

Source: who-malaria-qa

## Can someone have malaria and another illness at the same time?

Yes. A person can have malaria together with another infection, for example a child with both malaria and pneumonia. This is one reason a health worker may test for more than one illness.

Source: rti-safian-fever

## When does a fever need urgent medical attention?

Fever needs urgent care when it comes with danger signs such as confusion or being very hard to wake, convulsions, difficulty breathing, dark or bloody urine, yellowing of the eyes or skin, or abnormal bleeding. People with these severe symptoms should get emergency care right away.

Source: who-malaria
