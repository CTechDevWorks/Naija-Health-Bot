## What should I do about mosquitoes after heavy rain?

Once the rain has stopped, empty, turn over or cover anything around the home that has collected water, such as buckets, basins, old tyres and cans. Clear gutters so that water drains away. Check mosquito nets for holes and make sure everyone sleeps under one. Large flooded areas that are not draining should be reported to the local government health or environment office.

Source: nmep-ivm, who-lsm-manual, who-malaria

## What should I do about standing water near my home?

Small amounts of standing water can be poured out, drained or filled in with soil. Containers that hold water should be emptied or covered. For a pond, a blocked public drain or any water too large to deal with safely, ask the local government health or environment office for help. Do not treat the water with chemicals yourself.

Source: nmep-ivm, who-lsm-manual

## How should I store water at home so mosquitoes do not breed in it?

Keep water drums, tanks and buckets covered with a tight lid or a fine mesh, and close them again after each use. Empty and scrub containers that are not in use. Do not pour oil, kerosene or any chemical into water that is used for drinking, cooking or washing.

Source: who-lsm-manual, nmep-ivm

## What should I do about a blocked drain or gutter?

A blocked drain holds still water where mosquitoes can breed. If it is safe to do so, clear leaves and rubbish so that the water flows, wearing gloves and covered shoes. Do not climb into deep drains or pour insecticide into them. If you cannot clear it safely, report it to the local sanitation or environment office.

Source: nmep-ivm, who-lsm-manual

## What should my household do when malaria cases are high in our area?

Make sure everyone sleeps under a mosquito net every night, use mosquito repellent after dusk, and wear clothing that covers the arms and legs in the evening. If anyone in the home has a fever, get them tested for malaria promptly.

Source: who-malaria, who-malaria-qa

## How do I protect a young child or pregnant woman at home from malaria?

Infants, children under 5 and pregnant women are at higher risk of severe malaria, so protect them first. Make sure they sleep under a mosquito net every night. A pregnant woman should attend antenatal care, where preventive malaria treatment is given. Ask the immunisation clinic about the malaria vaccine for young children. If a young child or pregnant woman has a fever, get a malaria test and see a health worker the same day. Never give malaria medicine without a health worker's advice.

Source: who-malaria, who-malaria-qa
