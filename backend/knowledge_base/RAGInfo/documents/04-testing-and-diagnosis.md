## Why should you get a malaria test before taking malaria medicine?

The World Health Organization recommends that every suspected case of malaria is confirmed with a test that looks for the parasite before treatment. WHO recommends that malaria treatment is given only if a person tests positive. Early diagnosis and treatment of malaria reduces disease, prevents deaths and helps reduce the spread of malaria.

Source: who-malaria, who-malaria-qa

## What kinds of malaria test are there?

There are two main types of malaria test. One is microscopy, where a blood sample is examined under a microscope. The other is a rapid diagnostic test, often called an RDT. Both tests check for the presence of the parasites that cause malaria.

Source: who-malaria, who-malaria-qa

## Why does a test matter if the symptoms look like malaria?

Malaria symptoms are similar to those of many other illnesses that cause fever. Diagnostic testing lets health workers tell malaria apart from other causes of fever, so the person gets the appropriate treatment. Treating without a test can mean the real cause of the illness is missed.

Source: who-malaria, who-malaria-qa

## Can a rapid malaria test miss malaria?

Sometimes. Most rapid diagnostic tests for malaria look for one or two specific proteins made by the P. falciparum parasite. Parasites with certain genetic changes do not make these proteins and are not detected by these tests. WHO reports that such parasites had been found in 42 countries by 2024, although they are still uncommon in most countries.

Source: who-malaria

## What is Nigeria's policy on malaria testing?

Nigeria's National Malaria Elimination Programme aims for everyone who seeks care with suspected malaria to be tested, using either a rapid diagnostic test or microscopy, before treatment.

Source: nmep-strategic-plan-2021-2025
