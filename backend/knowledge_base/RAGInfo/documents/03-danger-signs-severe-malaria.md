## What are the danger signs of severe malaria?

Some types of malaria can cause severe illness and death. Severe symptoms of malaria include extreme tiredness and fatigue, impaired consciousness such as confusion or being very hard to wake, multiple convulsions, difficulty breathing, dark or bloody urine, yellowing of the eyes and skin (jaundice), and abnormal bleeding.

Source: who-malaria

## What should someone do if they see danger signs of severe malaria?

People with severe symptoms of malaria should get emergency care right away. Go to the nearest hospital or health centre immediately and do not wait to see whether the symptoms pass.

Source: who-malaria

## What other warning signs mean a sick person needs urgent care?

Other warning signs that need urgent medical attention include repeated vomiting, being unable to eat, drink or breastfeed, unusual weakness where the person cannot sit or stand, and very fast breathing. In a young child, any of these signs together with fever is an emergency.

Source: PENDING

## How does severe malaria affect children and adults?

Severe malaria can cause failure of several organs in adults. Children with severe malaria frequently suffer from severe anaemia (a dangerous shortage of healthy blood), breathing distress, or cerebral malaria, which affects the brain.

Source: who-malaria-qa

## Where is severe malaria treated?

Severe malaria needs care in a health centre or hospital. Most malaria medicines are in pill form, but some people need to go to a health centre or hospital for medicines given by injection.

Source: who-malaria

## Who is most likely to develop severe malaria?

Infants, children under 5 years, pregnant women, travellers and people with HIV or AIDS are at higher risk of severe malaria infection. Anyone in these groups who has a fever should be tested and seen by a health worker quickly.

Source: who-malaria
