## Who is at higher risk of malaria when travelling?

People who have no partial immunity to malaria are at higher risk of getting the disease. This includes travellers from countries without malaria, and also people within a malaria-affected country who live in an area with little or no malaria and travel to an area with high transmission.

Source: who-malaria-qa

## What should I do before travelling to an area with malaria?

See a doctor several weeks before you leave. The health professional will decide whether you need medicine to prevent malaria and which one suits the place you are going. Some preventive medicines have to be started before departure. You can also ask your national disease control centre which precautions apply to your destination.

Source: who-malaria, who-malaria-qa

## How should preventive malaria medicine for travel be taken?

Preventive malaria medicine for travel is prescribed by a doctor. It should be taken on schedule for the whole stay in the malaria risk area and continued after leaving for as long as prescribed, because parasites may still emerge from the liver after the trip. WHO advises that this is normally four weeks after the last possible exposure.

Source: who-malaria

## How can travellers avoid mosquito bites?

For travellers, sleeping under an insecticide-treated net is the most practical protection. Preventive medicine combined with an insecticide-treated net and repeated use of a mosquito repellent on the skin significantly lowers the risk of infection. Repellents containing DEET, IR3535 or icaridin are recommended after dusk.

Source: who-malaria, who-malaria-qa

## What if I get a fever during or after a trip to a malaria area?

Get tested for malaria promptly and tell the health worker where you travelled. Symptoms often do not appear until 10 to 15 days after infection, so a traveller may already be back home before feeling ill. Doctors in places without malaria may not recognise the symptoms, which can cause dangerous delays.

Source: who-malaria-qa

## Can the same medicine used for prevention treat malaria if I still get it?

If a person took preventive medicine and still gets malaria, the same medicine should not be used for treatment. Tell the health worker which preventive medicine you took so they can choose a different one.

Source: who-malaria-qa

## Do pregnant women and young children need extra care when travelling?

Yes. Pregnant women, infants and children under 5 are at higher risk of severe malaria. They should see a health professional before travelling to an area with malaria to decide on the right precautions.

Source: who-malaria
