## How serious is malaria in Nigeria?

Nigeria has the largest share of malaria deaths of any country. According to the World Health Organization, Nigeria accounted for about 31.9% of malaria deaths in the WHO African Region in 2024, and about 30.9% of malaria deaths worldwide in 2023.

Source: who-malaria, who-malaria-qa

## Which malaria parasite is most common in Nigeria?

Malaria in Nigeria is mainly caused by Plasmodium falciparum, which accounts for about 94% to 98% of infections. It is the most dangerous malaria parasite and is responsible for most severe cases. Plasmodium malariae accounts for almost 2% of infections and Plasmodium ovale is rare.

Source: nmep-strategic-plan-2021-2025

## Which mosquitoes spread malaria in Nigeria?

The main mosquitoes that spread malaria across all ecological zones of Nigeria are Anopheles coluzzii, Anopheles gambiae, Anopheles arabiensis and Anopheles funestus. The mix of these species varies between states and zones.

Source: nmep-strategic-plan-2021-2025

## Where in Nigeria is malaria transmission highest?

Malaria transmission is high across Nigeria. It is higher in rural communities and in communities beside major rivers and other bodies of water, such as irrigation dams in the northern parts of the country. Survey data showed malaria was more than twice as common among children in rural areas as in urban areas.

Source: nmep-strategic-plan-2021-2025

## How common is malaria among young children in Nigeria?

Between 2010 and 2018 the share of Nigerian children under 5 with malaria parasites fell from 42% to 23%. In 2018 it varied widely between states, from 52% in Kebbi State to 1.8% in Lagos State. By region it was highest in the North West at 33.8% and lowest in the South South at 15.6%. These are 2018 survey figures and newer surveys may differ.

Source: nmep-strategic-plan-2021-2025

## Who is most at risk of malaria in Nigeria?

In Nigeria, pregnant women and children are the groups most at risk from malaria.

Source: nmep-strategic-plan-2021-2025

## What is Nigeria doing to control malaria?

Nigeria's National Malaria Elimination Programme distributes insecticidal nets through mass campaigns and routine health services, plans indoor spraying in targeted areas, and uses management of mosquito breeding sites as a supporting measure. It provides preventive treatment to pregnant women at antenatal clinics and in the community, seasonal preventive treatment to eligible children, and testing and treatment for people with malaria.

Source: nmep-strategic-plan-2021-2025

## What is the goal of Nigeria's national malaria plan?

Nigeria's National Malaria Strategic Plan for 2021 to 2025 set a vision of a malaria-free Nigeria. Its goal was to bring the share of people with malaria parasites below 10% and to reduce deaths attributable to malaria to fewer than 50 per 1,000 live births by 2025.

Source: nmep-strategic-plan-2021-2025
