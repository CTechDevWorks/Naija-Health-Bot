## Why is malaria dangerous in pregnancy?

Pregnant women are at higher risk of severe malaria. Malaria infection during pregnancy can also cause premature delivery or the delivery of a baby with low birth weight. Plasmodium falciparum, the main malaria parasite in Nigeria, can heavily infect the placenta, which affects both the woman and the outcome of the pregnancy.

Source: who-malaria, nmep-strategic-plan-2021-2025

## How is malaria prevented during pregnancy?

The World Health Organization recommends intermittent preventive treatment of malaria in pregnancy for women living where malaria is common. In Nigeria this preventive treatment is offered to all pregnant women at antenatal clinics and at community level. It is given by health workers at set times during pregnancy, so attending antenatal care is the way to receive it. Pregnant women should also sleep under a mosquito net.

Source: who-malaria, who-malaria-qa, nmep-strategic-plan-2021-2025

## What should a pregnant woman do if she has a fever?

A pregnant woman with a fever should get a malaria test and see a health worker promptly, because pregnant women are at higher risk of severe malaria. Pregnancy is one of the things health professionals consider when choosing a malaria medicine, so she should tell the health worker that she is pregnant and should not take leftover medicine or medicine chosen without advice.

Source: who-malaria

## Should a pregnant woman tell the clinic about other medicines she takes?

Yes. A pregnant woman should tell the antenatal clinic about any medicines she takes regularly, including medicines for HIV, because this can affect which malaria prevention the health worker gives.

Source: PENDING

## Can a breastfeeding mother be treated for malaria?

Yes. A breastfeeding mother who has malaria can be treated. She should tell the health worker that she is breastfeeding so that a suitable medicine is chosen.

Source: PENDING
