## How can I protect myself from malaria?

Malaria can be prevented by avoiding mosquito bites and with medicines. The main ways to avoid bites are sleeping under a mosquito net, using mosquito repellent after dusk, using coils and vaporizers, wearing protective clothing, and fitting window screens.

Source: who-malaria

## How do mosquito nets protect against malaria?

Use a mosquito net when sleeping in places where malaria is present. Insecticide-treated nets prevent mosquito bites while people sleep and kill mosquitoes as they try to feed. Insecticide-treated nets are one of the two core tools the World Health Organization recommends for controlling the mosquitoes that spread malaria.

Source: who-malaria, who-malaria-qa

## Which mosquito repellents help prevent malaria?

The World Health Organization advises using mosquito repellents that contain DEET, IR3535 or icaridin after dusk. Use a repellent as directed on its label.

Source: who-malaria

## What else reduces mosquito bites at home?

Besides nets and repellents, WHO lists using coils and vaporizers, wearing protective clothing, and using window screens as ways to lower the risk of mosquito bites.

Source: who-malaria

## Are there medicines that prevent malaria?

Yes. Some malaria medicines are given to prevent the disease in people at highest risk. WHO recommends preventive treatment programmes for people living where malaria is common, including preventive treatment in pregnancy and seasonal malaria chemoprevention for young children during the period of greatest risk. These medicines are given by health workers through health programmes at set times, not bought and taken on one's own.

Source: who-malaria, who-malaria-qa

## Is one way of preventing malaria enough?

Malaria prevention works best when several measures are used together. WHO says the malaria vaccine has the highest impact when it is introduced alongside other recommended measures such as bed nets, and that preventive medicines are meant to add to mosquito control, prompt testing and treatment, not replace them.

Source: who-malaria
