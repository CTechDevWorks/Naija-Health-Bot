## What happens in the body after an infected mosquito bite?

After an infected mosquito bites, the malaria parasites travel to the liver, where they multiply without causing symptoms. They then enter the blood and infect red blood cells. This blood stage is what causes fever and the other symptoms of malaria.

Source: who-malaria, msd-malaria

## Can malaria come back after treatment?

Two types of malaria parasite, P. vivax and P. ovale, can cause relapses, and an extra medicine is added to the main treatment to prevent this. These types are uncommon in Nigeria, where P. falciparum causes about 94% to 98% of malaria infections.

Source: who-malaria, nmep-strategic-plan-2021-2025

## Why might someone get malaria again soon after being treated?

There are different reasons. Most often in Nigeria it is a new infection from another mosquito bite. Sometimes the first treatment did not clear all the parasites, for example if the course was not finished. Anyone whose symptoms return after treatment should go back to a health worker for another test.

Source: who-malaria-qa

## What is antimalarial drug resistance?

Drug resistance means malaria parasites are no longer cleared as well by a medicine. Partial resistance to artemisinin, where the parasites are cleared more slowly, has been confirmed in Eritrea, Rwanda, Uganda and Tanzania, and is suspected in Ethiopia, Sudan, Namibia and Zambia, according to the World Health Organization in December 2025. Studies generally show that ACTs still work well, and WHO asks countries to keep monitoring how well malaria medicines work.

Source: who-malaria, who-malaria-qa

## How can people help keep malaria medicines working?

ACTs are the most effective malaria medicines available, and no replacement is expected for several years, so they need to be protected. WHO recommends that malaria treatment is given only when a person has tested positive for malaria.

Source: who-malaria-qa
