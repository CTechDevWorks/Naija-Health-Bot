## Is there a vaccine for malaria?

Yes. The World Health Organization recommends two malaria vaccines for children: RTS,S/AS01, recommended since October 2021, and R21/Matrix-M, recommended in October 2023. They are for children living in areas with moderate to high transmission of P. falciparum malaria. Vaccination has been shown to significantly reduce malaria, and deadly severe malaria, among young children.

Source: who-malaria, who-malaria-qa

## Are malaria vaccines being given in Africa?

Yes. Malaria vaccines are being rolled out in routine childhood immunisation programmes across Africa. WHO expects them to save tens of thousands of young lives every year.

Source: who-malaria

## Does the malaria vaccine replace mosquito nets?

No. The malaria vaccine works alongside other protection. WHO says the highest impact is achieved when vaccines are introduced together with other recommended measures such as bed nets. A vaccinated child should still sleep under a net and be tested promptly if they have a fever.

Source: who-malaria

## Who gets the malaria vaccine and how many doses are there?

The malaria vaccines are for young children. They are given in four doses, starting from around 5 months of age. The immunisation clinic gives the dates for each dose, and all four are needed for the best protection. The vaccines are not currently given to adults or pregnant women.

Source: who-malaria-vaccines-qa

## How well do malaria vaccines work?

Malaria vaccines do not give complete protection. In studies they reduced malaria cases in young children by about half or more in the first year after vaccination, and protection was higher when doses were timed just before the rainy season in areas with strongly seasonal malaria.

Source: who-malaria-vaccines-qa

## Are malaria vaccines safe? Can the vaccine cause malaria?

WHO describes both malaria vaccines as safe and effective. The vaccines do not contain live malaria parasites and cannot cause malaria. Mild effects such as fever or soreness where the injection was given can happen. Serious reactions are rare.

Source: who-malaria-vaccines-qa

## Is the malaria vaccine available in Nigeria?

Nigeria has begun introducing malaria vaccines as part of childhood immunisation on a subnational basis, with plans described for wider national rollout. Availability changes by state over time, so ask at your local primary health centre or immunisation clinic for the current position.

Source: napharm-vaccines-nigeria, who-r21-nigeria
